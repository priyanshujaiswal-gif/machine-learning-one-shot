03 · What is it, exactly?

Learning from data.

Every prediction is one of two kinds.

RegressionPredicts a numberSalary, price, delivery time, sales next week.
Example modelsLinear RegressionDecision TreeRandom ForestGradient BoostingSupport Vector Regression
ClassificationPredicts a categorySpam or not. Fraud or not. Survived or not.
Example modelsLogistic RegressionDecision TreeRandom ForestK-Nearest NeighboursSupport Vector MachineNaive Bayes

Today: regression, live. Classification: your homework.

Game · Number or category?
Features

The clues

Columns the model looks at. Age, experience, education. Often called X.

Target

The answer

The column we want to predict. Salary. Often called y.

Model

The pattern-finder

A formula that turns clues into an answer. Training tunes it.

Step 5 of 8
Separate clues from the answer

    
What it isSplit the table in two: X (the features) and y (the target).
Why it mattersThe model needs to know which columns to learn from and which to predict. If the answer leaks into X, it cheats.

    
Try it

Step 6 of 8
Hide an exam

    
What it isSplit the rows: most to train on, the rest kept hidden to test on.
Why it mattersA model can memorise its training data. Only unseen data shows whether it truly learned.

Step 7 of 8
Pick a model. Train it.

    
What it isChoose a model type, then .fit() it. Training means nudging the model’s numbers until its errors shrink.
Why it mattersThis is the “learning”. Linear regression is the simplest model there is, and a great first one: it draws the best straight line through the data.

Test it. Use it.

    
What it isPredict on the hidden test rows, compare with the real answers, then predict for new people.
